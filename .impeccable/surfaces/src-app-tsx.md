## Direction contract

THESIS: The overlay is the landing's own painted signage: a fare board, a route line, small enamel placards. It refuses the category default of dark glass pills floating over WebGL.

OWN-WORLD: Bottle green, sign cream and painted ochre from the owner's approved sign vector, plus sun gold for focus. Square-cornered painted placards with an inner keyline. One heavy condensed grotesque for years and labels, the same family at normal width for reading. Only one opaque object: the cream facts board with a green header rail.

STORY: The visitor reads the sign, sees the year, drags to look, rides the route line through time, and opens the board to check sources.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Full-bleed scene. Top left: the sign wordmark in cream. Top right: language, sound, quality as quiet placards. Bottom left: the year at display size, era name under it, Facts beside it. Bottom right: Ride, Shore, Sky. Bottom edge: the true-scale route line with year stops. Text at the bottom sits on a green-black scrim; the top controls are opaque placards and need none.

SIGNATURE INTERACTION: Era change. The green field closes, the year counts through the years between the two eras in fixed digit cells, then the field opens on the new scene. On load, the route line draws itself from 1820 to 1986 and the stops land in order.

Raises from declined challengers. Seven-segment: digits live in fixed cells; nothing slides. Transit diagram: the timeline is a route diagram; phones keep the chosen stop centred, never shrink the line. Shader portal: type sits on living pixels; panels are the exception. Understory: after 7 s of stillness the placards fade and the scene stands alone; they wake on approach (mouse devices). Kiln shelf: every fact keeps its sources beside it. Merz: a pressed placard lifts and settles; no glide.
